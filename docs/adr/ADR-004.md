# ADR-004: Short-code generation: random base62, length 7

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted, superseded in part by ADR-017 |
| Date | 2026-09-29 |
| Decision owner | Madhu |
| Reversibility | High |

## Context

How should short codes be minted?

## Options considered

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Random base62, length 7 | 62^7 is about 3.5 x 10^12 values; collisions are vanishingly rare and handled by a bounded retry. |
| Base58 | Avoids visually ambiguous glyphs, at a small cost in space. Chosen later, as the brownfield change. |
| Hash of the target | Deduplicates automatically, but leaks that two callers shortened the same URL and makes per-link expiry awkward. |
| Monotonic counter | Shortest codes, but enumerable: anyone can walk the whole set. |

## Decision

**Random base62 of length 7**, from `SecureRandom`, with a bounded collision retry of 3 attempts.

## Rationale

A counter was rejected on privacy grounds rather than aesthetic ones: enumerable codes mean every link ever created is discoverable. The retry bound matters because with this key space repeated collisions mean something is actually wrong - an exhausted space or a broken random source - and looping forever would turn that into a hang instead of an error.

## Consequences

Codes are not guessable and not enumerable. The alphabet is injected rather than constant, which is what let ADR-017 change it later without touching call sites.
